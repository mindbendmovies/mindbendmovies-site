Collision Course Ending Explained: Who Gets the Real Diamonds — and What the Final Timeline Means

2026 • Thriller • Drama • Crime • 2h 2m • Streaming on Netflix
Updated 21 November 2026
⚠ Spoiler warning: this post explains the full ending of Collision Course (2026), including every timeline, who lives, who dies, and who ends up with the real diamonds. If you have not watched it yet, watch first — then come back.

Spain, 2013. After robbing a bank, a getaway crash reveals the hidden side of each thief, exposing betrayals and lies that began long before the heist.

Collision Course (original Spanish title Colisión), directed by Daniel Sánchez Arévalo and produced by Bambú Producciones, arrived on Netflix worldwide on 30 October 2026 — and shot to No. 1. Instead of one escape, it shows five: the film keeps rewinding the same crash to ask what happens if a different member of the crew dies.

Quick answer: Nobody in the crew really wins. The diamonds in safe deposit box 34 were fakes, swapped before the robbery as part of an insurance fraud worth about €5 million — the real stones end up with Ernesto, the bank manager who planned the heist, who flies to Bangkok with them. And the final version, where all four robbers survive the crash and drive away together, is not a happy ending: the same clues that destroyed them in the other timelines — Carla and Raúl's affair, the dog mess on Pedro's boot — are all still there, paused minutes before everything falls apart.

What Happens at the End of Collision Course?

Four masked robbers storm a Madrid bank branch to empty safe deposit box 34. But this is not a crew of career criminals: three of them are serving police officers — Carla (Laia Costa), Tomás (Tamar Novas) and Raúl (Álvaro Cervantes) — and the fourth is Pedro (Karra Elejalde), a retired officer and Raúl's father. Their inside man is Ernesto (Luis Zahera), the branch manager, who planned the robbery and told them box 34 holds diamonds. Because they work inside law enforcement, they expect to slow down their own colleagues' investigation and cover their tracks from within.

The plan collapses minutes into the escape. Their speeding getaway car collides with another car on the road, and from that moment the film stops moving forward in a straight line. Instead it rewinds to the crash again and again, playing out a different version each time — one for each crew member who dies, and a final one where nobody does. Each version forces a different secret into the open: an affair, a stolen stone, a boot print, a swapped diamond.

Version 1: If Pedro Dies

In the first version, Pedro is killed on impact, impaled by a crowbar in the crash. The others get the car away and burn it, but Pedro's body is identified by the police through the serial number on his knee prosthesis — a retired cop leaves a paper trail even in death. With his father gone, Raúl's grief curdles into panic: convinced the group is about to be exposed, he shoots Tomás at their hideout. The crew that was supposed to protect each other from the inside starts killing each other instead.

Version 2: If Raúl Dies

When Raúl is the one who dies in the crash, Pedro refuses to burn the getaway car, so that his son's mother can bury him properly. That decision is what undoes him: the police find the print of his old regulation boot in dog mess outside the bank, and the trail leads straight to the father. Meanwhile Pedro takes the stones he secretly removed from box 34 and hides them inside his son's coffin — but the stones are found, and Pedro is arrested. Without Raúl, the cyber-savvy officer who could have slowed the internal investigation, the remaining crew cannot hold their story together.

Version 3: If Carla Dies

Carla's death brings a different fracture. Inspector Luis (Lluís Homar) interrogates Tomás and tells him two things: Carla was pregnant when she died in the crash, and she was having a secret affair with Raúl. Tomás had sensed Carla had feelings for Raúl but never confronted her — now, convinced the child was Raúl's, he tracks Raúl down in a parking lot and guns him down in cold blood. When Pedro demands to know what happened to his son, Tomás shoots the old man too, and flees. In this version, the affair nobody would name becomes the thing that kills them all.

Version 4: If Tomás Dies — Who Gets the Real Diamonds?

The version in which Tomás dies is the one that finally explains the diamonds. With Tomás gone, the others stay calm long enough to follow the money — and the truth is that the heist was rigged from the start. In every version, Pedro quietly pocketed the stones from box 34. Here, when he tries to sell them, a pawnbroker tells him they are fakes: moissanite, a cheap imitation of diamond.

The real diamonds were swapped out before the robbery ever happened. The owner of box 34 had the genuine stones replaced with fakes and then claimed the insurance payout — reported at roughly five million euros — once the heist made the loss look real. Ernesto was in on it from the beginning: he arranged the robbery with the owner as an elaborate insurance fraud, using the crew as cover.

Ernesto's secret lover Lola, the café waitress, tries to double-cross him: she swallows the stones to smuggle them abroad and boards a different flight, meaning to punish the bank that ruined her brother. But she learns from Ernesto that the stones she is carrying are fakes too. Ernesto, who anticipated the move, flies to Bangkok with the real diamonds — while Pedro, Carla and Raúl are arrested at the terminal. The crew risked everything for a box that was empty before they ever opened it.

The Final Timeline: Nobody Dies — Is It a Happy Ending?

After the four fatal versions, the film returns to the crash one last time. This time, nobody dies. The four torch the getaway car, take another, and drive off together. In the back seat, Raúl and Carla trade a smile. At the wheel, Tomás complains about the smell, and Pedro tells him to get used to it.

That smell is the dog mess on Pedro's boot — the same print that put him in a cell in the version where his son died. And that smile is the affair that turned Tomás into a killer in the version where Carla died. Whatever they carried out of box 34 was fake before anyone pulled on a mask. The fifth version is not a reward for sitting through the other four; it is all of them at once, paused minutes before they go off. The film ends with the crew together and free — and with everything that will destroy them already in the car.

What Does the Ending Mean?

The story is set in 2013, in the hangover of Spain's financial crisis, and that setting is the point. Pedro joined the job after a yen-denominated mortgage wiped out his savings — a retired policeman who played by the rules and still lost everything. Each version of the crash turns on a different secret, but the secrets all come back to the same thing: who already owned the stones, and who was already lying. The crew thought they were stealing from a bank; in truth, the bank's own manager and a rich client had already stolen from the insurance company, and the crew were just the alibi. The film's trick is that the crash changes nothing that matters — the betrayals were all in place before the car ever moved.
